# Paper research notes

This is the research-method log for the foundation release and strength-training expansion. Stable citations and evaluated summaries belong in `source-index.md`; topic-level synthesis belongs in the relevant concept document.

## Search and selection method

The literature pass prioritized:

1. systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
2. consensus and position statements for health/nutrition boundaries;
3. direct interventions with useful runner-specific outcomes;
4. unusually informative observational studies when long-term interventions are impractical;
5. mechanistic and conceptual reviews when the construct itself required definition.

Primary locators were PubMed, PMC, DOI records, official journal pages, and official consensus pages. Search results, secondary summaries, social posts, and snippets were not treated as evidence records.

## Inclusion decisions

- Running was prioritized for economy, injury, durability, and performance.
- Mixed endurance-sport evidence was retained when the underlying question was central adaptation, intensity distribution, fueling, or cross-training transfer.
- Cycling studies were retained only with an explicit transfer/specificity boundary.
- Recent sources did not automatically displace older foundational sources.
- A source had to contribute a distinct claim, boundary, contradiction, or population—not simply increase the citation count.

## Evidence cautions applied

- Mileage-performance correlations were not converted into causal dose response.
- Elite practice was treated as feasibility among selected survivors.
- Acute molecular or metabolic responses were not converted into long-term performance effects.
- “No significant difference” in sparse cross-training evidence was not interpreted as proof of equivalence.
- Fresh-state economy and VO2max were kept separate from their deterioration during prolonged exercise.
- LT1, LT2, ventilatory thresholds, MLSS, fixed blood-lactate values, and critical speed were not treated as aliases.
- Evidence that strength improves economy was not used to claim generic injury prevention.
- Evidence above 90 g/h carbohydrate was separated by sport, sex, sample size, preparation, tolerance, metabolism, and performance outcome.
- General-adult resistance prescription was not treated as runner-performance evidence.
- High-load resistance, power work, plyometrics, isometrics, and high-repetition circuits were kept as distinct methods.
- Fresh economy, fatigued economy, time to exhaustion, time trial, race performance, tissue properties, pain, and injury incidence were kept as distinct outcomes.
- Minimal development dose and reduced maintenance dose were not treated as synonyms.
- Null runner trials were retained when strength or muscle outcomes improved without a fresh running-performance effect.

## High-priority gaps

- Months-long randomized volume-progression studies in novice and trained runners.
- Female-specific and sex-comparative evidence across training ages.
- Standardized durability interventions rather than cross-sectional associations.
- Running-specific cycling addition/substitution trials with tissue, economy, and performance outcomes.
- Marathon-like comparisons of 60, 90, 100, and 120 g/h across sexes and environmental conditions.
- Long-term energy-availability studies with bone-stress injury and performance endpoints.
- Better running-specific strength/tissue-capacity injury-prevention trials.
- Runner-specific comparisons of heavy resistance, plyometrics, and combined methods with equalized total cost.
- Maintenance-dose studies after strength and economy gains in trained runners.
- Strength-placement trials that measure both the target run session and long-term adaptation.
- Bone- and tendon-loading studies across sex, life stage, energy availability, and running-training age.

## Maintenance rule

When adding a paper, register it once in `source-index.md`, state the claim for which it is being weighted, and update the relevant synthesis. Do not append a paper merely because it agrees with the current conclusion.
