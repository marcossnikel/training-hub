# Source index

This file is the canonical registry for source IDs used throughout the repository.

## Entry schema

Each entry records:

- full title and citation;
- source/publication and year;
- source type;
- population, sample size, and intervention duration when relevant;
- major findings and major limitations;
- evidence weight for the claims used here;
- DOI, PubMed, PMC, or canonical URL;
- access date and peer-review status;
- coach/practice status and related scientific sources when applicable.

No source is included merely to reach a target count. Evidence weight is claim-specific; an entry can be strong for one narrow claim and weak for another.

## Scientific sources

### [SRC-001] Endurance exercise performance: the physiology of champions

- **Citation:** Joyner, M. J., & Coyle, E. F. (2008). *The Journal of Physiology*, 586(1), 35–44.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed integrative review; human running and cycling literature with an elite-performance emphasis; no single sample or intervention.
- **Finding / limitation:** Organizes endurance performance around VO2max, sustainable fraction, and economy/efficiency. It is a conceptual model, not proof of a session or program.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong conceptual foundation; indirect for prescription.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.2007.143834](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2007.143834); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17901124/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-002] Effects of exercise training on mitochondrial and capillary growth in human skeletal muscle

- **Citation:** Mølmen, K. S., Almquist, N. W., & Skattebo, Ø. (2025). *Sports Medicine*, 55(1), 115–144.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-regression; 5,973 participants; 353 mitochondrial and 131 capillary articles; interventions of varying length.
- **Finding / limitation:** Continuous, high-intensity, and sprint formats can increase mitochondrial content and capillarization; baseline fitness and training exposure strongly moderate percentage change. Very heterogeneous populations and protocols; not a runner-specific dose prescription.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong for broad skeletal-muscle adaptation; moderate for modality/intensity comparisons; limited for elite programming.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02120-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02120-2); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39390310/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-003] Under the Hood: Skeletal Muscle Determinants of Endurance Performance

- **Citation:** van der Zwaard, S., Brocherie, F., & Jaspers, R. T. (2021). *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, 3, 719434.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed integrative review with meta-analytic components; endurance-athlete and human muscle literature; no single intervention.
- **Finding / limitation:** Integrates fiber oxidative capacity, oxygen supply, fiber size/type, capillarization, and myoglobin with whole-body determinants. Many underlying associations are not causal training trials.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong conceptual and mechanistic synthesis; indirect for exact programming.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.3389/fspor.2021.719434](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2021.719434); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34423293/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-004] Training periodization, methods, intensity distribution, and volume in highly trained and elite distance runners

- **Citation:** Casado, A., González-Mohíno, F., González-Ravé, J. M., & Foster, C. (2022). *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance*, 17(6), 820–833.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review; 10 studies of highly trained and elite middle- and long-distance runners; observational periods varied.
- **Finding / limitation:** Elite practice is usually pyramidal, with most work below LT1 and smaller proportions near and above LT2. Describes selected survivors; does not establish causal superiority or safe recreational doses.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate observational evidence for elite practice; limited for prescription.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2021-0435](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2021-0435); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35418513/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-005] Aerobic high-intensity intervals improve VO2max more than moderate training

- **Citation:** Helgerud, J., Høydal, K., Wang, E., Karlsen, T., Berg, P., Bjerkaas, M., Simonsen, T., Helgesen, C., Hjorth, N., Bach, R., & Hoff, J. (2007). *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 39(4), 665–671.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed randomized trial; 40 healthy, moderately trained men; four work-matched running programs, three sessions weekly for eight weeks.
- **Finding / limitation:** 15/15 and 4 × 4-minute intervals improved VO2max more than continuous moderate or threshold groups; economy did not change. Short, male-only, and not a full seasonal running program.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate for short-term VO2max response; limited for long-term distribution.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1249/mss.0b013e3180304570](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e3180304570); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17414804/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-006] Lactate threshold concepts: how valid are they?

- **Citation:** Faude, O., Kindermann, W., & Meyer, T. (2009). *Sports Medicine*, 39(6), 469–490.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed critical review; 25 threshold concepts and 32 performance-validation studies; no intervention duration.
- **Finding / limitation:** Threshold methods often correlate with endurance performance but target different phenomena and do not show reliable individual interchangeability. The synthesis is older and largely validation-oriented.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong conceptual evidence that operational definitions matter; moderate validation evidence.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.2165/00007256-200939060-00003](https://doi.org/10.2165/00007256-200939060-00003); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19453206/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-007] The measurement and application of critical speed and D-prime in running

- **Citation:** Anderson, M., Sabapathy, S., Arnold, B., Bellenger, C., Kolodziej, F., & Bellinger, P. (2026). *Sports Medicine*, 56(7), 1637–1674.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed scoping review; 124 running studies; test protocols and study duration varied.
- **Finding / limitation:** Critical speed is useful as a heavy–severe boundary and performance marker, but model and protocol materially affect the estimate; D-prime evidence is thinner. Association does not prove superior CS-based training.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate for measurement/application; limited for prescription superiority.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-026-02410-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-026-02410-x); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41931241/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-008] The maximal metabolic steady state: redefining the “gold standard”

- **Citation:** Jones, A. M., Burnley, M., Black, M. I., Poole, D. C., & Vanhatalo, A. (2019). *Physiological Reports*, 7(10), e14098.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed integrative physiological review; constant-work, MLSS, and critical-power/speed literature; no single sample.
- **Finding / limitation:** MLSS commonly yields a lower intensity than critical power/speed and should not be treated as synonymous. The preferred “gold standard” remains debated and much evidence is cycling-based.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate mechanistic evidence for non-equivalence; contested on hierarchy.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.14814/phy2.14098](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.14098); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31124324/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-009] The importance of durability in physiological profiling

- **Citation:** Maunder, E., Seiler, S., Mildenhall, M. J., Kilding, A. E., & Plews, D. J. (2021). *Sports Medicine*, 51(8), 1619–1628.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed conceptual review of endurance physiology; no pooled intervention.
- **Finding / limitation:** Defines durability as the onset and magnitude of deterioration in physiological characteristics during prolonged work. Early framework, no standardized test, and limited causal training evidence.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong conceptual evidence; limited empirical prescription evidence.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01459-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01459-0); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33886100/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-010] Physiological resilience: what is it and how might it be trained?

- **Citation:** Jones, A. M., & Kirby, B. S. (2025). *Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports*, 35(3), e70032.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed integrative review; endurance literature; no pooled intervention.
- **Finding / limitation:** Extends the classic model to within-event deterioration and proposes long-term volume, prolonged sessions, late intensity, and strength as candidates. The authors explicitly note that intervention data are scant.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong conceptual synthesis; limited/hypothesis-level for specific training formats.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1111/sms.70032](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.70032); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40024804/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-011] Changes in running economy and attainable VO2max after prolonged running

- **Citation:** Unhjem, R. J. (2024). *Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports*, 34(5), e14637.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed controlled observational study; 10 trained runners and 10 active adults; one hour at 70% VO2max.
- **Finding / limitation:** Trained runners showed less economy deterioration and preserved attainable VO2max better at the same relative intensity. Small sample and one acute protocol.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate for training-status differences under this protocol; not causal for a specific training method.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1111/sms.14637](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14637); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38671555/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-012] Regular long runs and higher training volumes are associated with better running-economy durability

- **Citation:** Zanini, M., Folland, J. P., & Blagrove, R. C. (2026). *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 58(1), 162–173.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed performance-matched cross-sectional study; 26 well-trained men; 90-minute run at lactate threshold.
- **Finding / limitation:** Long-run group had less economy deterioration; durability correlated with longest run and weekly volume. Cross-sectional, male-only, small sample, and residual self-selection.
- **Evidence weight:** Limited-to-moderate observational evidence for association; hypothesis-level for causation.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003840](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003840); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40878015/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-013] Strength-training programs and running economy

- **Citation:** Llanos-Lagos, C., Ramirez-Campillo, R., Moran, J., & Sáez de Villarreal, E. (2024). *Sports Medicine*, 54(4), 895–932.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis; 31 controlled studies, 652 middle- and long-distance runners; 6–24 weeks.
- **Finding / limitation:** High-load training produced a small economy benefit; some combined methods and plyometrics also helped in defined conditions. Heterogeneous small studies; economy is not injury prevention or guaranteed race improvement.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate for small high-load/plyometric economy effects; lower for combined methods.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165636/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-014] Running biomechanics and running economy

- **Citation:** Van Hooren, B., Jukic, I., Cox, M., Frenken, K. G., Bautista, I., & Moore, I. S. (2024). *Sports Medicine*, 54(5), 1269–1316.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies; 51 studies, 1,115 healthy adults; acute testing.
- **Finding / limitation:** Isolated biomechanical variables explained only 4–12% of between-runner economy variation; many popular variables were trivial or inconsistent. Observational associations are not automatic retraining targets.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate observational evidence; limited causal evidence.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-01997-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-01997-3); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38446400/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-015] Changes in training load and running-related injury

- **Citation:** Damsted, C., Glad, S., Nielsen, R. O., Sørensen, H., & Malisoux, L. (2018). “Is there evidence for an association between changes in training load and running-related injuries? A systematic review.” *International Journal of Sports Physical Therapy*, 13(6), 931–942.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review; only four eligible prospective studies; follow-up and load definitions varied.
- **Finding / limitation:** Evidence for sudden load-change thresholds was sparse and inconsistent. Too few heterogeneous studies to validate a universal safe percentage.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate evidence that certainty is low; insufficient evidence for an exact progression rule.
- **Locator:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6253751/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-016] Running injuries and training parameters

- **Citation:** Fredette, A., Roy, J.-S., Perreault, K., Dupuis, F., Napier, C., & Esculier, J.-F. (2022). *Journal of Athletic Training*, 57(7), 650–671.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review of 36 prospective articles; 23,047 runners and 6,043 injuries; follow-up varied.
- **Finding / limitation:** Associations involving distance, duration, frequency, intensity, and recent change were conflicting. Definitions, populations, and exposure measures were highly heterogeneous.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate evidence against a simple universal load rule; limited for any exact threshold.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.4085/1062-6050-0195.21](https://doi.org/10.4085/1062-6050-0195.21); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34478518/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-017] Training-volume and frequency changes before the Boston Marathon

- **Citation:** DeJong Lempke, A. F., Ackerman, K. E., Stellingwerff, T., Burke, L. M., Baggish, A. L., d'Hemecourt, P. A., Dyer, S., Troyanos, C., Saville, G. H., Adelzadeh, K., Holtzman, B., Hackney, A. C., & Whitney, K. E. (2026). *Sports Medicine*, 56(1), 243–256.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed observational study; 917 Boston Marathon athletes (495 women, 422 men); pre-race training and frequency-change period.
- **Finding / limitation:** Training volume/frequency patterns were associated with race performance. A qualified, self-selected cohort and observational design prevent causal mileage prescriptions.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate observational evidence for association; limited causal evidence.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02304-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02304-4); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40913707/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-018] Training-intensity distribution of marathon runners across performance levels

- **Citation:** Muniz-Pumares, D., Hunter, B., Meyler, S., Maunder, E., & Smyth, B. (2025). *Sports Medicine*, 55(4), 1023–1035.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed observational big-data study; 16 weeks preceding 151,813 marathons by 119,452 runners.
- **Finding / limitation:** Faster runners accumulated more training, primarily at low intensity, with distribution differences across performance levels. App-derived data, inferred zones, selection, and confounding preclude causal conclusions.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong descriptive scale; moderate observational evidence; limited prescription evidence.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02137-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02137-7); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39616560/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-019] Training determinants of marathon performance

- **Citation:** Doherty, C., Keogh, A., Davenport, J., Lawlor, A., Smyth, B., & Caulfield, B. (2020). *Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport*, 23(2), 182–188.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review, meta-analysis, and univariate meta-regression; 85 studies and 137 cohorts; elite through recreational marathoners.
- **Finding / limitation:** Weekly distance, frequency, peak week, longer runs, training pace, and hours correlated with faster marathons. Aggregate observational data are confounded by ability, training age, genetics, recovery, and selection.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate observational evidence for association; limited for individual causal dose response.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2019.09.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2019.09.013); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31704026/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-020] Nutrition and athletic performance position statement

- **Citation:** Thomas, D. T., Erdman, K. A., & Burke, L. M. (2016). *Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics*, 116(3), 501–528.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed joint professional position statement; athletes across sports and competition contexts; no single sample.
- **Finding / limitation:** Supports individualized energy, carbohydrate, protein, fluid, recovery, and supplement strategies. Broad rather than running-specific; the evidence base and numeric certainty vary by recommendation.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong consensus for adequacy and contextual planning; claim-specific for numeric targets.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26920240/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-021] Carbohydrates for training and competition

- **Citation:** Burke, L. M., Hawley, J. A., Wong, S. H. S., & Jeukendrup, A. E. (2011). *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 29(Suppl 1), S17–S27.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed evidence-based review; endurance and team-sport studies; no single intervention.
- **Finding / limitation:** Establishes duration- and demand-scaled carbohydrate availability, including multiple transportable carbohydrate at high rates. Much underlying work is cycling and male-dominant; pre-dates recent 100–120 g/h work.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong guideline foundation for established ranges; limited above 90 g/h and for every runner.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1080/02640414.2011.585473](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2011.585473). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-022] Acute carbohydrate feeding and endurance performance

- **Citation:** Ramos-Campo, D. J., Clemente-Suárez, V. J., Cupeiro, R., Benítez-Muñoz, J. A., Andreu-Caravaca, L., & Rubio-Arias, J. Á. (2024). *Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition*, 64(30), 11196–11205.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review, meta-analysis, and meta-regression; 136 endurance studies; acute exercise tests.
- **Finding / limitation:** Carbohydrate improved performance on average, with larger effects in longer events and time-to-exhaustion tests. Heterogeneity and test selection prevent inference of one optimal dose.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong for average acute benefit; limited for a universal grams-per-hour target.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1080/10408398.2023.2233633](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2023.2233633); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37449467/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-023] Gut-training and feeding-challenge systematic review

- **Citation:** Martinez, I. G., Mika, A. S., Biesiekierski, J. R., & Costa, R. J. S. (2023). *Sports Medicine*, 53(6), 1175–1200.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review; eight cycling, running, and intermittent-exercise studies; 4–28 days.
- **Finding / limitation:** Some repeated feeding exposures reduced discomfort or malabsorption, while symptom, integrity, and function results were inconsistent. Small, heterogeneous evidence base.
- **Evidence weight:** Limited-to-moderate for trainable tolerance; insufficient for one canonical protocol.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-023-01841-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01841-0); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37061651/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-024] Periodized carbohydrate restriction in endurance-trained athletes

- **Citation:** Gejl, K. D., & Nybo, L. (2021). *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, 18, 37.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis; nine studies in well-trained endurance athletes; at least one week and restricted-carbohydrate exposure at least three times weekly.
- **Finding / limitation:** No overall performance benefit over normal carbohydrate availability; interval-quality reduction is a plausible cost. Few heterogeneous studies across cycling, triathlon, and race walking.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate against a general performance advantage; limited for specific periodized subgroups.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1186/s12970-021-00435-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-021-00435-3); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34001184/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-025] 2023 IOC consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport

- **Citation:** Mountjoy, M., Ackerman, K. E., Bailey, D. M., Burke, L. M., Constantini, N., Hackney, A. C., Heikura, I. A., Melin, A., Pensgaard, A. M., Stellingwerff, T., Sundgot-Borgen, J. K., Torstveit, M. K., Jacobsen, A. U., Verhagen, E., Budgett, R., Engebretsen, L., & Erdener, U. (2023). *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 57(17), 1073–1098.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed IOC expert consensus and evidence review; female and male athletes across sports; incorporates more than 170 newer publications.
- **Finding / limitation:** Problematic low energy availability can affect multiple health and performance systems in both sexes; introduces revised models and CAT2 risk stratification. Measurement is error-prone and no universal diagnostic biomarker or cutoff exists.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong consensus for multi-system risk; outcome- and diagnostic-specific certainty varies.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37752011/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-026] Running/cycling cross-training and transfer

- **Citation:** Menges, T., Dindorf, C., Dully, J., & Fröhlich, M. (2026). *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, 8, 1843803.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis of seven randomized trials; trained or recreationally active participants; at least four weeks.
- **Finding / limitation:** No statistically detected between-modality differences for pooled VO2max or 1-mile to 5,000-m running outcomes, but confidence intervals were wide. Sparse, heterogeneous, often old studies do not prove equivalence.
- **Evidence weight:** Limited; supports possible aerobic transfer, not interchangeability.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1843803](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2026.1843803); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42267259/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-027] Physiological differences between cycling and running

- **Citation:** Millet, G. P., Vleck, V. E., & Bentley, D. J. (2009). “Physiological differences between cycling and running.” *Sports Medicine*, 39(3), 179–206.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed integrative review; runners, cyclists, and triathletes; acute and training studies.
- **Finding / limitation:** VO2max, heart rate, ventilatory response, economy, and local fatigue differ by modality and training history. Older literature and heterogeneous testing limit exact zone conversion.
- **Evidence weight:** Strong conceptual evidence for specificity; moderate for modality differences; limited for exact prescriptions.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.2165/00007256-200939030-00002](https://doi.org/10.2165/00007256-200939030-00002). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-028] Which training-intensity distribution produces the greatest improvement?

- **Citation:** Rosenblat, M. A., Watt, J. A., Arnold, J. I., Treff, G., Sandbakk, Ø. B., Esteve-Lanao, J., Festa, L., Filipas, L., Galloway, S. D., Muñoz, I., Ramos-Campo, D. J., Schneeweiss, P., Sellés-Pérez, S., Stöggl, T., Talsnes, R. K., Zinner, C., & Seiler, S. (2025). *Sports Medicine*, 55(3), 655–673.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed systematic review and individual-participant-data network meta-analysis; 13 studies, 348 endurance athletes; short heterogeneous interventions.
- **Finding / limitation:** No overall polarized-versus-pyramidal difference for VO2max or time-trial performance. Exploratory training-status interactions require confirmation; men and mixed modalities dominate.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate-to-strong against universal polarized superiority; limited for subgroup effects.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02149-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02149-3); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39888556/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

### [SRC-029] Training session models in endurance sports: a Norwegian perspective

- **Citation:** Tønnessen, E., Sandbakk, Ø., Sandbakk, S. B., Seiler, S., & Haugen, T. (2024). *Sports Medicine*, 54(11), 2935–2953.
- **Type / population / duration:** Peer-reviewed pragmatic qualitative best-practice study; 12 experienced male Norwegian coaches across eight Olympic endurance sports, responsible for athletes with more than 370 international medals.
- **Finding / limitation:** Sessions were high-volume, progressive, controlled rather than exhaustive, and modality-specific; moderate-intensity sessions exceeded high-intensity sessions annually. Expert-practice reconstruction, no causal comparison, male coach sample.
- **Evidence weight:** Moderate for documenting elite Norwegian practice; limited for superiority or recreational transfer.
- **Locator:** [DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02067-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02067-4); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39012575/). Accessed 2026-08-25.

## Coaching and methodology sources

Coach sources can establish what a coach says or does. They cannot, by themselves, establish mechanism, safety, or causal superiority.

### [SRC-030] The Norwegian model of lactate-threshold training

- **Citation:** Bakken, M. (2022). “The Norwegian model of lactate threshold training and lactate controlled approach to training.”
- **Type / population / duration:** Original coach/athlete history; Bakken's elite running experiments and retrospective practice, including more than 5,500 self-reported lactate measurements over years; not peer reviewed.
- **Finding / limitation:** Documents the empirical origin, lower controlled lactate targets, interval accumulation, clustering, and “X element.” First-person reconstruction with no control group; Bakken explicitly says it is not a scientific article.
- **Evidence weight:** Primary coach-practice source; hypothesis/experience for effectiveness.
- **Locator:** [Canonical article](https://www.mariusbakken.com/the-norwegian-model.html). Accessed 2026-08-25. Related science: [SRC-006], [SRC-008], [SRC-028], [SRC-029].

### [SRC-031] Double Threshold Training

- **Citation:** Bakken, M. (2026). “Double Threshold Training.”
- **Type / population / duration:** Coach/athlete explainer and historical synthesis; advanced distance-running practice; not peer reviewed.
- **Finding / limitation:** Clarifies session separation, intensity control, and the role of double threshold inside a larger system. Updated practice account, not a randomized comparison of clustered versus distributed work.
- **Evidence weight:** Primary methodology source; limited direct evidence for superiority.
- **Locator:** [Canonical article](https://www.mariusbakken.com/double-threshold-training.html). Accessed 2026-08-25. Related science: [SRC-029].

### [SRC-032] Semi-structured easy/moderate runs
