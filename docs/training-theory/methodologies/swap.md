# SWAP coaching methodology

## Definition and source boundary

SWAP (Some Work, All Play), associated with David and Megan Roche, is a coaching methodology. It combines frequent easy running, small exposures to speed, one or more purposeful quality stimuli, autoregulated terrain-based work, strength, cross-training, aggressive attention to fueling, and recovery.

There is no direct controlled trial of “SWAP” as a complete system. This page therefore maps coach claims to proposed mechanisms and independent evidence. It does not grade the Roches' athlete outcomes as causal proof.

Much SWAP material is trail/ultra oriented. Each principle below marks road applicability.

## Reconstructed framework

### 1. “Get fast and stay fast”

SWAP uses strides, hill strides, short hills, and periodic faster work across training cycles rather than confining speed to a short race phase. [SRC-036]

**Proposed mechanism:** neuromuscular recruitment, speed familiarity, economy, mechanical ceiling.\
**Evidence:** Direct evidence for strides alone is limited; high-load/plyometric strength has moderate support for small economy gains. [SRC-013]\
**Limitations:** Fast running has low metabolic duration but high local force.\
**Road applicability:** High, when terrain and dose are road-relevant.\
**Status:** **Coach practice + mechanistic hypothesis**.

### 2. Most running easy or very easy

SWAP often describes most running below the first threshold, with deliberately slow starts and recovery responsive to stress. [SRC-038]

**Proposed mechanism:** repeatable oxidative volume at low fatigue cost.\
**Evidence:** Elite and intervention literature supports high proportions of low intensity, but not one universal percentage or HR cap. [SRC-004][SRC-028]\
**Limitations:** “Easy” measured by pace, HR, lactate, and RPE can disagree; beginners may reach moderate intensity at any running pace.\
**Road applicability:** High.\
**Status:** **Moderate independent support for the principle; exact execution contextual**.

### 3. Easy/moderate and “running with intention”

In an authenticated 2023 Patreon post, the Roches describe easy/moderate long runs that start very easy and allow smooth, terrain-driven progression when the athlete feels good. They warn that forced moderate work on tired legs can become counterproductive and describe the approach as especially trail-specific. [SRC-032]

**Proposed mechanism:** aerobic-threshold output, type-I recruitment, economy at sustainable speed, and terrain-specific mechanics.\
**Evidence:** Moderate-intensity work is a real physiological domain; direct trials of this autoregulated SWAP format are absent.\
**Limitations:** On hills, time near threshold can be much greater than the session label suggests. With two structured workouts, it may create excessive middle intensity.\
**Road applicability:** Moderate; a controlled steady or progressive run is more transferable than trail downhill/uphills.\
**Status:** **Coach practice**.

### 4. Threshold and one-hour effort

SWAP uses “one-hour effort,” threshold, and moderately hard work as practical cues rather than laboratory synonyms.

**Proposed mechanism:** high oxidative flux and improved sustainable output.\
**Evidence:** Threshold-domain training is physiologically credible, but one-hour pace, LT2, MLSS, and critical speed are not interchangeable. [SRC-006][SRC-008]\
**Limitations:** Duration proxies vary with athlete level, terrain, weather, and fatigue.\
**Road applicability:** High if the operational cue is explicit.\
**Status:** **Evidence-informed coaching interpretation**.

### 5. Long runs and fatigue resistance

SWAP uses long runs, easy/moderate terrain, fueling, and occasional late-session efforts to develop fatigue resistance. A 2025 Patreon synthesis labels much of the specific intervention theory as conjectural; the 2026 “fatigue resistance snack” is explicitly presented as an experiment with confounding. [SRC-034][SRC-035]

**Proposed mechanism:** economy durability, sustained muscular recruitment, late-session carbohydrate oxidation, and nervous-system adaptation.\
**Evidence:** Durability is a valid construct; regular long runs and volume are associated with better economy durability. The causal value of post-run steep treadmill plus high-rep step-ups is untested. [SRC-009][SRC-012]\
**Limitations:** Trail downhills and ultra duration add eccentric and terrain-specific demands not required for road marathon.\
**Road applicability:** The general construct is high; exact trail/ultra tools are low-to-moderate.\
**Status:** **Mixed: observational support for long-run exposure; hypothesis for the snack/package**.

### 6. Progressive mileage with autoregulation

SWAP encourages aerobic build weeks and volume growth but warns that some athletes get slower or unhealthy under high volume.

**Proposed mechanism:** cumulative oxidative and musculoskeletal development.\
**Evidence:** Mileage-performance associations are moderate and strongly confounded; injury-progression rules are uncertain. [SRC-016][SRC-019]\
**Limitations:** No universal maximum productive volume or validated 10% rule.\
**Road applicability:** High.\
**Status:** **Evidence-compatible coaching framework, not a dose rule**.

### 7. Strength as a compact support layer

SWAP commonly uses short strength routines, step-ups, lunges, bands, core work, and occasional heavier exercises. Across the Roche articles reviewed here, the recurring priority is adherence: keep a non-zero background dose that an endurance athlete can repeat without overflowing the total “stress cup.” [SRC-055][SRC-056][SRC-057][SRC-058]

**Proposed mechanism:** force capacity, economy, tissue loading, adherence, and local muscular endurance.\
**Evidence:** Moderate for runner economy and performance from high-load/combined methods; strong general-adult evidence that many resistance prescriptions improve strength; insufficient to claim generic running-injury prevention. [SRC-013][SRC-043][SRC-045][SRC-049]\
**Limitations:** High-repetition frequent step-ups are not equivalent to the high-load methods best supported for economy. The general-population minimal-dose review does not directly validate Roche's exact endurance-athlete routine. [SRC-053][SRC-055]\
**Road applicability:** High, with method-specific reasoning.\
**Status:** **Evidence-informed coach practice; exact dose and injury claims remain separate**.

#### Strength-source map

| SWAP source | What it documents | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal-dose resistance article | Non-zero adherence, low logistical barrier, stress tradeoff | General evidence supports minimal doses; runner extrapolation is indirect [SRC-053][SRC-055] |
| “Should You Strength Train?” | Brief functional routines and skepticism about excessive lifting | Coach interpretation; some 2021 claims are narrower than newer high-load runner evidence [SRC-056] |
| Mountain Legs | Frequent high-repetition rear lunges and step-ups for uphill support | Exact exercise/dose untested; local-endurance coach practice [SRC-057] |
| Core Snack | A 2–3-minute personal core routine repeated during the day | Explicitly anecdotal, not presented by Roche as leg-strength-level evidence [SRC-058] |
| Hamstring exercise | Brief single-leg isometric hold used from personal experience | Anecdotal/rehab hypothesis; not diagnosis or treatment evidence [SRC-059] |
| Bone-density article | Resistance and additional weight-bearing loading inside a broader medical discussion | General loading idea has support; exact package and clinical claims are not endorsed [SRC-052][SRC-060] |

The evolution matters. The 2021 article emphasizes short functional work and warns heavy lifting could detract. Newer runner syntheses find the clearest economy/performance signal from high-load resistance. The repository preserves both: Roche's adherence and stress-management logic is useful, while intervention choice should follow the target adaptation rather than coach identity. [SRC-045][SRC-046][SRC-056]

### 8. Cross-training should develop the modality

SWAP's 2026 bike framework emphasizes raising cycling power and muscular endurance rather than only passive spinning. [SRC-033]

**Proposed mechanism:** a better cyclist can generate a larger aerobic stimulus before local fatigue becomes limiting.\
**Evidence:** Cycling can transfer central fitness, but evidence is sparse and does not validate universal running HR zones, >85 rpm, or two rides per week. [SRC-026][SRC-027]\
**Limitations:** Cycling fatigue can interfere with running; specificity remains.\
**Road applicability:** Moderate as a complementary layer.\
**Status:** **Coach practice with partial mechanistic support**.

### 9. Fuel the work

SWAP strongly promotes carbohydrate during long and demanding sessions and cautions against chronic fasted training. [SRC-032][SRC-037]

**Proposed mechanism:** maintain carbohydrate oxidation and output, support recovery, and reduce low-energy-availability risk.\
**Evidence:** Strong for carbohydrate's average endurance-performance benefit and strong consensus concern about problematic low energy availability in both sexes. [SRC-020][SRC-022][SRC-025]\
**Limitations:** Exact grams per hour and the health impact of one fasted easy run depend on context; avoid converting a strong safety orientation into a universal claim about every session.\
**Road applicability:** High.\
**Status:** **Strong independent support for adequate/contextual fueling; specific universal prohibitions require nuance**.

### 10. Minimum effective stress and joy/autoregulation

SWAP prioritizes sustainable work, play, rest, and adjusting effort to how the athlete feels.

**Proposed mechanism:** improved consistency and lower unproductive fatigue/opportunity cost.\
**Evidence:** The general adaptation-fatigue logic is coherent; the specific SWAP implementation has not been experimentally isolated.\
**Limitations:** Autoregulation can become uncontrolled moderate work without honest feedback and clear weekly intent.\
**Road applicability:** High.\
**Status:** **Coaching philosophy**.

## Example weekly logic, not a prescription

The 2023 Patreon example clusters one primary workout and a hilly easy/moderate long run, with easy running, strides/hill strides, optional cycling, strength, and a rest day around them. [SRC-032]

The reusable principle is load separation:

- protect truly easy recovery;
- retain low-dose speed;
- make the long run purposeful but not automatically hard;
- add cross-training only when it does not erode running;
- fuel the demanding work.

The exact weekdays, frequency, and effort labels are not universal.

## Where SWAP is strongest

- It treats athlete response and joy as information rather than noise.
- It keeps speed development present while emphasizing low-intensity volume.
- It explicitly discusses stimulus and fatigue.
- Its modern fueling emphasis aligns with stronger evidence than many depleted-training traditions.
- Its public/private writing often acknowledges hypotheses and uncertainty.

## Where evidence is weakest

- Direct effects of frequent strides/hill strides on economy and injury.
- Exact easy/moderate intensity distribution.
- Neural explanations for fatigue-resistance snacks.
- Universal cycling HR/cadence/power rules.
- Transfer of trail/ultra tools to road marathon.
- The complete methodology versus alternative systems.
- The exact performance and injury effects of SWAP's short, frequent strength routines.

## Road versus trail/ultra applicability

| Principle | Road | Trail/ultra note |
|---|---|---|
| Easy foundation and speed maintenance | High | Also high |
| Terrain-driven easy/moderate | Moderate | High and highly terrain-specific |
| Fast downhills for eccentric resilience | Low for flat road racing | High where race descents dominate |
| Long-run fatigue resistance | High for marathon | High, but ultra duration/logistics differ |
| Cycling as added load | Moderate | Often attractive for ultra aerobic volume |
| 50k-effort cues | Low unless operationally translated | Higher within ultra coaching context |

## Open questions

- Do frequent low-dose strides improve economy beyond normal quality training?
- Can autoregulated easy/moderate runs be quantified without destroying their purpose?
- Which SWAP fatigue-resistance interventions change a standardized durability measure?
- Does modality-focused cycling add more running benefit than equal-time easy cycling?
- When does SWAP's minimal strength dose maintain adherence but underdose maximal force or power?
- How do frequent Mountain Legs contacts interact with hills, sprints, and downhill load?
